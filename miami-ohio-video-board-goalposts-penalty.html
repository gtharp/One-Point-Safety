Miami (OH) Flagged for Fake Goalposts on Its Video Board: NCAA Rule 9-2-1 Explained

One-Point Safety · November 13, 2026

As Ohio's kicker lined up a 34-yard field goal at Yager Stadium, Miami's video board lit up with extra goalposts right behind the real ones. The officials threw a flag on the home team for unsportsmanlike conduct. The board did it, not a player. It's one of the strangest penalties of the 2026 FBS season.

Game
Ohio at Miami (OH), Tuesday, Nov. 10, 2026, at Yager Stadium in Oxford, Ohio
Final
Miami (OH) 34, Ohio 17
When
2nd quarter, 0:06 left before halftime
Play
Ohio 34-yard field goal try, from the Miami 17
Foul
Unsportsmanlike conduct on Miami (OH): a distracting video board display
Rule
NCAA Football Rule 9-2-1
Penalty
Half the distance to the goal (17 → 8), automatic first down, kick wiped out
Result
Ohio got no points from the drive

What happened

Ohio had driven inside the Miami 20 with seconds left in the first half. As the Bobcats lined up for the field goal, Miami's video board behind that end zone showed a graphic of extra field goal uprights, placed so that from the kicker's angle they sat right behind the real posts. It's an optical trick: give the kicker more than one set of uprights to look at and hope he lines up wrong.

The kick missed. Some reports say it clanged off an upright. But a flag was already down. The referee announced unsportsmanlike conduct on Miami, citing the display of field goal uprights on the scoreboard. Because the foul was on the defense, Ohio could take the penalty and wipe out the miss, and it did.

The play, as logged

This is the official play-by-play entry, exactly as it shows up in our college play log for week 11:

(00:06) #43 B.Peacock field goal attempt from 34 yards NO GOOD , clock 00:02 PENALTY MIA UNS: Unsportsmanlike Conduct 9 yards from MIA17 to MIA08, 1ST DOWN. NO PLAY

Notice the "9 yards." An unsportsmanlike conduct foul is normally 15 yards, but the line of scrimmage was the Miami 17. A 15-yard walk-off would go past the goal line, so the officials enforced half the distance to the goal instead, which moved the ball from the 17 to the 8. The foul also carried an automatic first down.

What NCAA Rule 9-2-1 says

Rule 9-2-1 is the college rulebook's list of unsportsmanlike acts. Most of it covers players and coaches: taunting, baiting, delayed or excessive celebrations. One part reaches past the people in uniform. Bands, and the people running a stadium's audio, video and lighting systems, may not make noise or create a distraction that keeps a team from hearing its signals or obstructs play.

The foul goes against the team, the same as if a player had committed it. That means the person at the video board controls can cost the home team 15 yards (or half the distance) and an automatic first down. The NCAA rules committee updated Rule 9-2-1 before the 2026 season to change how unsportsmanlike fouls are enforced, with an emphasis on officials using good judgment. A display built to confuse a kicker is about as clear-cut a case as that judgment will get.

Why this one is so odd

  • Nobody on the field did anything wrong. The foul came from a graphic, not from a player, coach or band.
  • It almost never gets flagged. Fans and bands try to distract kickers every week. A flag on the stadium's own video board is close to unheard of.
  • It probably backfired. The kick missed anyway. Without the flag, the half ends with Ohio getting nothing. With it, Ohio got a first down at the 8 instead.
  • The penalty itself was unusual. A half-the-distance enforcement on a field goal try, with a few seconds left, on a foul by a team's own scoreboard.

Has this happened before?

The best-known case is from October 31, 2020. Oklahoma State's huge video board in Stillwater put up vertical yellow bars that looked like goalposts while Texas kicker Cameron Dicker lined up a 40-yard field goal just before halftime. No flag was thrown, and Dicker's kick squeaked inside the left upright. Afterward, the Big 12 reportedly told Oklahoma State the tactic was "not permissible," and Texas coach Tom Herman said the issue "has been addressed."

The difference six years later: this time the officials flagged it on the spot.

Frequently asked questions

Why was Miami (OH) penalized for its video board?

The board showed extra goalposts behind the real uprights while Ohio lined up a 34-yard field goal with six seconds left in the first half. Officials ruled it a distraction and flagged Miami for unsportsmanlike conduct under Rule 9-2-1.

What is NCAA football Rule 9-2-1?

The rule that lists unsportsmanlike acts. Part of it bars bands and audio, video and lighting operators from creating noise or a distraction that keeps a team from hearing its signals or obstructs play. The foul is charged to the team.

Can a scoreboard or video board draw a penalty in college football?

Yes, though it's very rare. Video board operators are covered by Rule 9-2-1, so a distracting display can be flagged as unsportsmanlike conduct against the home team.

Did Ohio score after the penalty?

No. Ohio got a first down at the Miami 8 but came away without points. All 17 of Ohio's points came on earlier drives, and Miami (OH) won 34-17.
